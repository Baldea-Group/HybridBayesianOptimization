Here are the core guidelines for writing and maintaining an effective voice in scientific and technical papers:

### Core Writing Guidelines
*   **Adopt a "Newspaper" Structure:** Organize your paper so the most important findings (the "punchline") appear up front, filling in details later for those who continue reading.
*   **Write with Conciseness:** Omit every unnecessary word. Use short, declarative sentences to keep your thoughts in order and make them easier to read.
*   **Simplify Your Language:** Use the **smallest, simplest word** that does the job; for example, use "use" instead of "utilize" and "several" instead of "diverse".
*   **Avoid "Throat-Clearing":** Do not start your paper with long, philosophical motivations or generic histories. Instead, write a "dynamite" introduction that explains the problem and your solution immediately. 
*   **Eliminate Redundancy:** Avoid repeating yourself; if you have said something once, you do not need to say it again in a different way.
*   **Integrate Equations Carefully:** Treat equations as part of your sentences, following them with proper punctuation. When referring to an equation, identify it by a phrase as well as a number (e.g., "the electric field E in Eq. 5") so the reader does not have to hunt for its meaning.
*   **Prioritize Editing:** Understand that **90% of writing is actually re-writing**. Finish a draft early enough to put it down and return to it with a fresh eye later.

### Voice and Tone Guidelines
*   **Embrace the Active Voice:** Use first-person, active voice (e.g., "We found..." or "I assume...") rather than the passive voice ("It was found..."). Active voice is clearer, more direct, and prevents you from hiding responsibility for your claims.
*   **Be Reader-Centric:** Treat your reader as a **guest in your house** by anticipating their needs and keeping their level of knowledge in mind at all times. 
*   **Commit to Scrupulous Honesty:** Maintain a voice of integrity by being honest about your method's drawbacks and reporting results even when they do not work as expected.
*   **Maintain a Gracious Tone:** Be kind and respectful to your peers; write about competing methods from a position of security rather than competition. 
*   **Avoid Superlatives and Intensifiers:** Skip "weak intensifiers" like "very," "strikingly," "interestingly," or "extremely". Let your facts and results carry the story rather than using adjectives to tell the reader how to feel.
*   **Be Humble:** Avoid self-praise and grandiosity. Write to explain something important, not to express how great you are.
*   **Take Responsibility:** Use "I" for sole-authored papers and avoid the "royal we". Take a deep breath and take full responsibility for what you are writing.
